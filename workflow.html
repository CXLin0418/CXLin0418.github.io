From paper
to Lean.

A compiler can check the theorem you wrote. How do you keep track of whether it is the theorem the paper meant?

Project

Lean workflow skills

My formalization workflow gives an AI assistant a sequence of jobs it can do with a paper and a Lean project. It starts by pinning down the mathematical claim. Each dependency gets a place in the argument and, eventually, a place in the code.

The $workflow skill coordinates source review, preparation, proof development, and handoff. Two companion skills, paper-extract and paper-target, reconstruct the argument and locate the declarations a project can already use.

Read the argument

Record the paper version, theorem label, hypotheses, and exact conclusion. When the paper skips an inference, mark the gap and keep its proposed repair visible.

Map it into Lean

Trace each supporting result to the step that uses it. Check types and applications against the project’s pinned Lean and mathlib versions, then record the target statement before starting its proof.

Prove one obligation

Work on a statement whose prerequisites are ready. Read Lean’s goal after each step and check that later declarations can use the finished theorem.

Review the result

Compare the final declaration and its assumptions with the recorded target. Inspect axiom dependencies, preserve the build evidence, and identify the next open obligation.

A small example

Where does a bound go?

Suppose a paper has established b = a and a ≤ M. The next line uses b ≤ M. Writing out the two steps shows exactly where each hypothesis enters.

example (a b M : Nat) (same : b = a)
    (bound : a ≤ M) : b ≤ M := by
  rw [same]
  exact bound
